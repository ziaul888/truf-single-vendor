# Finish the front desk before adding charts

Turf-admin should add **booking-workflow features, not more dashboard widgets**: no-show marking, a pre-play reminder email, staff rescheduling, recurring team bookings, booking from the calendar, a working notification bell, and one activity page covering all staff actions. The home page already has the "today at a glance" board that competitors market, and Reports already goes past the v1 analytics limit, so new charts would add least and break scope most. The largest gaps show up when bookings change: a paid booking cannot be moved to another slot, a regular team cannot be booked as a series, and a team that never turned up is recorded as "completed". Four of the recommended items need little or no API work because the data already exists, and four need turf-api changes. The evidence supports a direction, not a measured payoff: no operator interviews or independent turf benchmarks were found, the open-source turf projects have few stars, and most turf-specific claims come from vendors. Two owner decisions matter most: whether cash and dues are officially in v1, and whether the venue can hold a Stripe account at all.

## The basics competitors sell are already built

Commercial products share a recognisable core: a resource calendar, revenue reporting, staff-created bookings, slot blocks, role-based access and customer records ([Playtomic schedule](https://helpmanager.playtomic.com/hc/en-gb/articles/20560058451985-Schedule-basics), [CourtReserve schedulers](https://help.courtreserve.com/en/collections/9210267-schedulers-on-admin-panel), [Bookteq G-Cloud listing](https://assets.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud-15/documents/705623/806807843973490-service-definition-document-2026-01-29-1550.pdf)). The feature counts behind that pattern are floors set by how much each vendor documents, not market shares. Turf-admin covers all of it. **Every admin section calls the real turf-api**, and the only clear stub in the UI is the topbar bell (`src/components/topbar.tsx:40-42`). The home page has five attention cards, a per-ground slot timeline showing who is playing now and next, inline payment collection, a cash panel and an activity feed (`src/app/(admin)/dashboard/page.tsx`).

It also compares well with the closest open-source projects. [oyun's partner home](https://github.com/devcnisham/oyun/blob/main/src/app/partner/%28dashboard%29/page.tsx) computes similar daily figures, and [TurfHub's payments page](https://github.com/rahat9016/turf-booking-management/blob/main/src/components/turf/admin/Payments.tsx) has the same to-collect idea. But oyun's own tracker marks its payment gateway and notifications "Not started" ([FEATURE-LIST](https://github.com/devcnisham/oyun/blob/main/docs/FEATURE-LIST.md)), and TurfHub stores its data in localStorage ([store.ts](https://github.com/rahat9016/turf-booking-management/blob/main/src/lib/turf-store/store.ts)). Treat these repositories as a source of patterns, not as products to catch up with.

The strongest patterns to borrow are already in the code. I checked turf-api directly because the notes recommended these as new work without having reviewed the backend. **Double-booking is enforced by the database**: `Booking.liveSlotId` is unique and is cleared on cancellation, which gives the same result as [oyun's partial unique index](https://github.com/devcnisham/oyun/blob/main/supabase/migrations/20261006000000_oyun_store.sql#L41) (`turf-api/prisma/schema.prisma:333`, `turf-api/src/bookings/booking-cancellation.service.ts:55`). Checkout holds expire through a background job. A payment that arrives after its slot has gone is handled in the webhook, which marks the booking cancelled and refunded (`turf-api/src/bookings/stripe-webhook.service.ts:96-111`). The server CSV already neutralises spreadsheet formulas, like [oyun's export](https://github.com/devcnisham/oyun/blob/main/src/app/partner/%28dashboard%29/bookings/export/route.ts) (`turf-api/src/reports/csv.ts:4-10`). `cancelReason` and `cancelledById` also exist. None of this needs rebuilding. There is one small hole: the customers page's browser-side CSV only escapes quotes (`src/components/features/customers/customer-display.ts:62`), so a customer name starting with `=` would reach the spreadsheet unprotected. Reuse the server's rule there.

## Eight additions close the gaps staff will hit first

These features fit the v1 scope and rank by operational value against cost. The priorities and effort sizes are my judgement, based on a static read of `turf-admin` and `turf-api` without running either.

**No-show marking comes first because later decisions depend on it.** `BookingStatus` has no such state, and `completeEnded()` turns every ended confirmed booking into `completed` each hour (`turf-api/src/bookings/booking-jobs.ts:66-78`). The Completion Rate in Reports therefore counts absent teams as completed, so it does not measure attendance. Published rates are too far apart to borrow: restaurants at [2.33%](https://resos.com/restaurant-no-show-rate/), a clinic at [23.1%](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20569761/), and an unsourced vendor claim of [30–40%](https://turfbox.in/blog/turf-pricing-strategy-india-2026.html) for unpaid off-peak turf bookings. The venue has to measure its own. I recommend a nullable marker on the booking plus a new event type rather than a fifth status, so the hourly job and the existing status reports keep working. Marking must not trigger a charge or a refund, because the business rule allows only full refunds on cancellation. A list filter and a raw count stay within scope. A reported no-show rate does not.

**The reminder email has the best evidence of any item, though it comes from another sector.** In a randomized trial of 9,835 patients, automated reminders cut no-shows from 23.1% to 17.3%, and cancellations rose in both reminder arms ([Parikh et al. 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20569761/)). That trial used telephone reminders, and no randomized email evidence was found, so the size of the effect may not transfer. The direction suits a turf, because a cancelled slot can be resold and a silent no-show cannot. Include a cancel link and send the email before `cancellationCutoffHours` passes, otherwise it offers no way to release the slot. Expect the cancellation rate to rise, and do not read that as failure. The current `EmailKind` list has no such type (`src/types/settings.ts:79-81`), but the hourly job and the sent-at pattern from `dueReminderSentAt` can be reused. Booking cards could also expose a tap-to-call list for unpaid bookings today, since staff calls beat automated ones in the same trial (13.6%).

**Reschedule is a gap the notes did not call out.** `UpdateBookingSchema` accepts only `notes`, `dueDate` and `dueNote` (`turf-api/src/bookings/bookings.schemas.ts:109-113`), and a search of turf-api finds no reschedule logic. Moving a team today means cancelling and rebooking, which for card payments means a refund and a new charge. Build a "move to slot" dialog first, relying on the `liveSlotId` constraint to reject conflicts and writing a new booking event. Calendar drag-and-drop can wait. It drew [a specific complaint against Upper Hand](https://capterra.com/p/151570/Upper-Hand/reviews/) and appears in CourtReserve, but it adds notification and undo paths, which [Easy!Appointments' drag handler](https://github.com/alextselegidis/easyappointments/blob/develop/assets/js/utils/calendar_default_view.js#L559) has to handle explicitly. The owner must set one rule: what happens when the new slot has a different price, given that peak pricing exists.

**Recurring bookings are the largest build and the most turf-specific.** A vendor's unsourced claim says [70%+ of volume is the same teams week after week](https://clutchr.in/blog/turf-business-profitability-guide). A [Daily Star report](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/news/lost-playgrounds-booming-business-3869101) on Bangladeshi turfs quotes one operator saying about 80% of clients are corporate workers playing at night, and a [council block-booker form](https://enfield.gov.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0029/86582/3G-football-pitches-new-block-booker-application-2025-26-Leisure-and-culture.pdf) shows season hire as a formal process. Turf-api has no recurring-booking code outside slot blocks. Copy the behaviour of the existing recurring block, which skips booked slots and reports them, and link the bookings with a series ID so each one still cancels and refunds on its own. Keep this staff-only in v1, with payment recorded per booking. That choice depends on the cash decision below.

The smaller items mostly connect things that already exist. Month and week grids only select a date, and the day panel only links to existing bookings, so a free slot should open `bookings/new` with the ground and slot filled in. That is the shortest possible walk-in flow, and it matters because off-system bookings are the main remaining double-booking risk once one calendar is in place. A related bug: the customer page links to `bookings/new?customer=`, but I found no code on that page reading the parameter. The bell should show attention counts on every page, since the cards live only on the home page; it stays in-app and raises no SMS issue. The activity page is the most under-appreciated item. `BookingEvent`, `SlotEvent` and `SettingsEvent` already record who did what, so **no new audit table is needed**, contrary to what the notes suggested. Failed logins are the one thing not modelled. No commercial product in the sample was shown to have a staff audit log, though that may reflect thin documentation, and [Bookit's impressive-looking audit page](https://github.com/Kopano-Labs/Bookit-5s-Arena/blob/main/app/admin/audit/page.jsx) runs on hard-coded mock data. With two roles sharing refunds and cash, "who cancelled this" is a question the venue can already answer in data but not on one screen.

## Dashboard reliability and cheap unused capability come before anything new

A live money board that looks healthy while failing is worse than a missing feature. Attention cards and Today's Money show a skeleton on `isLoading || !data`, so **a failed request looks like loading forever**, and failed charts render as empty (`attention-cards.tsx:385`, `today-money.tsx:910`, `dashboard-charts.tsx:490,520`). KPI cards do not refresh while attention cards refresh every 60 seconds, so a page left open at the desk can show numbers that disagree (`src/hooks/useDashboard.ts:15-29`). The admin area has no `error.tsx` or error boundary, which CLAUDE.md requires. This work should ship first.

Several capabilities are already paid for but unused. The bookings hook accepts `from`, `to` and `sort`, yet the list page has no date filter (`src/hooks/useBookings.ts:15-26`). Front-desk staff need that filter, and the change is small. Reports accepts custom dates and `groupBy`, yet the pages offer only presets, even though Export already allows custom ranges. Exposing that adds no new metric. Replacing the status radial chart with bars follows [NN/g's guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive/) on quick comparison at no scope cost. Four inconsistencies are worth a quick cleanup: the "Add Ground" quick action shows to managers who then hit NoPermission, Staff > Roles says reports are admin-only when the API allows managers, `/bookings/confirmed` actually holds the pending queue, and the README promises PDF invoices while `jspdf` has no imports.

## Most of what competitors add is out of scope or not worth it yet

The v1 exclusions rule out a long list of competitor features outright: promo codes, loyalty and referrals, SMS and WhatsApp reminders, partner and owner mobile apps, wallets, bKash and Nagad, and multi-location or commission tools ([BookYourTurf](https://mwm.ai/apps/bookyourturf-partner/6763787322), [Bookit](https://github.com/Kopano-Labs/Bookit-5s-Arena/blob/main/README.md), [TurBD](https://github.com/Azad-2025/TurBD)). SMS has the strongest reminder evidence of any channel ([Resos](https://resos.com/restaurant-no-show-rate/)), which makes it the first exclusion worth revisiting after v1.

**The owner KPI view is the hardest call, and it stays out under the stated scope.** Vendors converge on utilization by weekday and hour plus revenue per available hour ([EZFacility](https://www.ezfacility.com/blog/sports-facility-management-metrics/), [Skedda](https://support.skedda.com/en/articles/5807358-insights), [Playtomic reports](https://helpmanager.playtomic.com/hc/en-gb/articles/43127115602961-Reports-in-Playtomic-Manager)). The data exists, and turf-api already calculates ground utilization with blocked slots removed from the denominator (`turf-api/src/reports/reports.service.ts:139-146`), which is Skedda's approach rather than Playtomic's. It is still analytics beyond a daily/weekly revenue CSV, so a heatmap, RevPAH, dues aging and repeat share should wait for an explicit waiver. The counter-argument is real: Reports already breaks the rule, and one vendor calls weekday daytime ["the desert every owner knows"](https://clutchr.in/blog/turf-business-profitability-guide), though nobody has measured what it costs. If the owner waives the rule, RevPAH is one column in an existing table and the heatmap is the next thing to build. Do not display external targets, because every turf benchmark found is an unsourced vendor figure. Compare against the venue's own trailing weeks instead.

Several features are not excluded but do not earn a place in v1. Memberships appear in eight of the fourteen products, yet they bring billing rules and sit close to the loyalty exclusion, and a recurring series covers the core need of regular teams. Court waitlists have one direct example ([CourtReserve](https://help.courtreserve.com/en/articles/11164239-new-admin-guide)), and [Omnify's](https://www.getomnify.com/solutions/facilities) is for programs rather than slots. QR check-in and kiosks ([box_cricket](https://github.com/niyati34/box_cricket), [MATCHi](https://playmore.matchi.com/en/matchi-insights/shaping-the-racket-sports-ecosystem-through-technology)) are heavier than a staff no-show button, so measure first. Expense and salary tracking appears only in [very new apps with almost no ratings](https://mwm.ai/apps/turf-manager/6766165177). Leagues, reviews, webhooks, calendar sync and editable email templates solve problems nothing here shows this venue has. Printable receipts and invoices are the nearest miss: [Hudle](https://hudle.in/list-your-sports-venue) and Bookteq list invoicing, so add them only if customers actually ask.

## Four owner answers change the plan

**Are cash, bank transfer and dues officially in v1?** CLAUDE.md excludes multiple payment methods, but the code records cash and bank transfer and tracks partial payments, and most desk value depends on them. Turf-focused apps are built around [dues and cash-in-hand](https://mwm.ai/apps/turf-manager/6766165177). If the answer is yes, add a **cash-recorded-per-staff view**, which turns the vendor-reported complaint of [no trail for staff cash](https://turfbox.in/blog/online-turf-booking-app-cricket-grounds.html) into a simple report over `Payment.recordedById`, currently unused by reports or the dashboard. I would treat that as a reconciliation control rather than analytics, though the owner may read the rule differently. If the answer is no, the collect flows and items 4 and 5 need redesigning around Stripe Checkout links.

**Can this venue use Stripe?** The repo's currency and timezone point to Bangladesh, and [Stripe's availability page](https://stripe.com/global) does not list it. This is a launch risk larger than any dashboard feature, so confirm the account's country and legal entity first.

**Does one surface sell as full, half or 5-a-side?** [Pitchbooking](https://www.getapp.com/all-software/a/pitchbooking/) splits facilities, and [USFA's scheduler](https://usfaevents.com/helpcenter.aspx?id=31) links a full field to its halves so that a booking on one blocks the other. The slot model's `@@unique([groundId, date, startHour])` has no way to express that link. If this venue sells split pitches, the change is a data-model priority above everything in the table. If not, ignore it. A related question: Bangladeshi operators [often sell 90-minute sessions](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/news/lost-playgrounds-booming-business-3869101), while `SLOT_DURATIONS` allows only 60 or 120 minutes.

**Does CLAUDE.md still describe the product?** It says Next.js 14 and Express, flat pricing and a revenue-CSV analytics limit. The code runs Next 16 and NestJS, with peak pricing and full Reports. Until someone updates it or freezes those areas, every scope judgement, including those in this report, rests on a document the code has already moved past.

## Conclusion

The research started as a comparison of dashboard features, but the useful gaps turned out to be missing states and actions in the booking lifecycle: absent, moved, repeating. A dashboard can only display what the system records, so no-show marking matters more than any widget a competitor shows. It also makes the numbers already on screen more accurate, and it gives the owner evidence for the later questions on pay-at-venue limits, late-cancellation policy and SMS, which are otherwise decided by guesswork.

The sensible check on this plan is to spend an afternoon with the venue's front-desk staff before building items 3 and 4. Everything said here about what operators need is inferred from vendor listings and code, and no source measured how often teams reschedule or rebook weekly. The bookings already in the database can partly answer that today: count cancel-and-rebook pairs by the same customer, and repeated weekday-and-hour patterns per customer. That query costs little and could reorder the top of the list.
